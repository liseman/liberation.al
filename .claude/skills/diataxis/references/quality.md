# Quality in documentation

"Quality" is a word in danger of losing its meaning. We all want it and rarely specify
what we mean. Yet we can point to high-quality documentation when asked, identify lapses
when we see them, and usually agree when we do. Diátaxis sharpens that grasp with a
distinction between **functional quality** and **deep quality**.

## Functional quality

Documentation must meet standards of **accuracy, completeness, consistency, usefulness,
precision** and so on. Fail any one of these and it fails a key function.

These properties are **independent of each other**. Documentation can be accurate without
being complete. It can be complete but inaccurate and inconsistent. It can be accurate,
complete, consistent, and also useless.

Attaining functional quality means meeting high, objectively measurable standards across
several independent dimensions, consistently. It demands discipline, attention to detail,
and technical skill. And every failure is immediately apparent to the user.

## Deep quality

Functional quality is not enough, or even satisfactory on its own as an ambition. True
excellence implies characteristics not covered by accuracy and completeness:

- feeling good to use
- having flow
- fitting to human needs
- being beautiful
- anticipating the user

These cannot be checked or measured, but they can be identified. We usually recognise them
when we meet them.

## The difference

| Functional quality | Deep quality |
|---|---|
| independent characteristics | interdependent characteristics |
| objective | subjective |
| measured against the world | assessed against the human |
| a condition of deep quality | conditional upon functional quality |
| aspects of constraint | aspects of liberation |

Documentation can satisfy every functional demand and still be awkward and unpleasant to
use. Meanwhile the deep qualities are hard to disentangle from one another: *having flow*
and *anticipating the user* are aspects of each other.

Functional aspects can be *measured*, literally with numbers in some cases. Deep qualities
can only be *enquired into*. Instead of taking measurements, we make judgements.

Deep quality is **conditional upon** functional quality. Nobody experiences documentation
as beautiful if it is inaccurate, or enjoys how it anticipates their needs if it is
inconsistent. The moment you hit a lapse, the experience is tarnished.

And they feel different to produce. Every functional characteristic appears to the author
as a **burden and a constraint**, a test that can be failed, and one that must be re-passed
after every release. Anticipating needs and creating flow are **liberation**: the work of
creativity and taste. To attain functional quality we must conform; to attain deep quality
we must invent.

## How deep quality is recognised

Consider clothing. It must keep you warm and dry and survive wear: objectively measurable,
and no expertise needed to judge. Other qualities cannot be measured. Judging that a
garment hangs well, moves well, or has been expertly shaped takes a developed eye.

But someone with no eye for it at all still recognises that the clothing is excellent,
because it **feels good to wear**, because they want to wear it. Their body knows.

The same is true of documentation. You may need to be a connoisseur to say *what* makes
some documentation excellent, but not to know *that* it is. Good documentation feels good.
You feel satisfaction using it; it fits and moves with you.

Users may lack the vocabulary to say why it is good or where it lapses. That does not
matter: it will feel good, or not. But **we, as creators, need a clear understanding of
what makes documentation good**, so we recognise what is good about it and not merely that
it is, and so we understand how people will feel using it. Producing work of deep quality
depends on it.

## What Diátaxis can and cannot do

**Cannot:** deliver functional quality. Accuracy and completeness come from domain
knowledge, discipline, and testing against the product. No framework substitutes for that.

**Can:**

- **Expose** lapses in functional quality, by making it obvious what each page is supposed
  to be doing and therefore what is missing or wrong.
- **Create** conditions for deep quality, by aligning each document with a real need, so
  the reader is never fighting the page.
- **Preserve flow**, because a document that serves one need does not interrupt itself.

Diátaxis is not a short-cut. It lays the conditions in which deep quality becomes
possible.

## Using this as a review lens

Two passes, in order. The second is wasted effort if the first fails.

**Pass 1, functional:**
- Is it accurate against the current version?
- Is it complete for the surface it claims to cover?
- Is it internally consistent in terminology, formatting, and structure?
- Is it precise, or does it hedge where it should state?
- Is it useful to anyone in particular?

**Pass 2, deep:**
- Does it anticipate the reader's next question, or make them go looking?
- Does it have flow, or does it stall, backtrack, and switch context?
- Does it fit the reader's actual situation, or an imagined ideal one?
- Would a competent reader feel respected by it, or talked down to?
- Does it feel good to use?

Answer pass 2 as a judgement, not a score. If you cannot say why something feels wrong,
say that it does, and say where.
